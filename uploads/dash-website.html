Dash Network — the layer underneath everything

One company can send orders and take orders

On Dash, sending and taking are directions rather than roles, so a company can face either way and change its mind by the hour. A Dash DMS fleet with idle drivers takes work in. The same fleet at 6pm, fully booked, pushes work out. Two switches, one account, neither affecting the other.

What that is worth

  • You stop turning business away. Overflow leaves your fleet and still gets delivered — by another Dash DMS fleet, a Dash 3PL, or a Dash Freelancer driver.
  • You keep the customer. Dash Network delivers the order and leaves the merchant, the pricing and the account exactly where they were.
  • You fill the quiet hours. Open the other direction and orders from Dash Merchant clients across the network come to you, without a salesperson.
  • You can stop any time. Each direction is a separate approval and a separate switch.

The network is the reason Dash is a hub and not a dashboard. Orders that one company cannot carry become work for another, automatically, without either of them negotiating anything.

Talk to us →

What actually happens

An order enters the network and is normalised — pickup, drop-off, timing, constraints. The engine filters everyone who could physically do it, ranks the ones who should, and offers it out. If nobody takes it, the fallback rules decide what happens next.

  • Coverage, capability and capacity filter first
  • Performance history ranks what is left
  • Failure at any stage has a defined next step

Company

A hub, not another delivery company

Dash does not own trucks. It connects the people who have orders with the people who can move them, and it runs the software both sides need to do that well.

Where we stop

Dash is business to business. We report pickup, transit and delivery to the merchant, and the merchant handles their own customer. There is no Dash app for the person receiving the parcel, and there will not be one.

Why both sides

Software and network belong together. The software is what a delivery business runs on day to day; the network is what it reaches for when the day does not go to plan. Building both means a company can be a customer today and capacity tomorrow.

What we are building toward

A routing engine good enough that a merchant stops thinking about delivery entirely — and a network deep enough that it always has an answer.
